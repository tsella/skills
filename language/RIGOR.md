# Evidentiary Rigor – No Conflation, No Inflation

**(non-negotiable)**

A real error occurred and must not recur. A whole-company number was silently used as a product-line proxy, inflating the evidence for consumer demand. Every contributor (including subagents) must follow these rules: Subagent sessions do not inherit this file; whoever dispatches one passes it in the child's context.

1. **Match the number to the exact claim.** A total (company, market, category) is NOT a proxy for a part (a product line, a segment, a use case). Before citing a figure, ask: "does this number measure the specific thing my sentence claims?" If it measures a *superset*, say so explicitly and do not let it stand in for the subset. Name the scope on every figure (e.g. "Signify parent total, ~64% professional lighting" – never "Hue's €6B business"). Match period and unit with the same test: a fiscal year is not a calendar year, gross revenue is not net revenue, and a figure in one currency, geography, or unit is not a proxy for another. A year-over-year change shows both dates.

2. **Population ≠ demand ≠ addressable buyers.** "X people play games / own TVs / bought smart bulbs" is reach, not validated willingness to pay for *this* product at *this* price. Adjacent-category revenue is context, not proof of demand for a category that doesn't exist yet. Label these as what they are.

3. **Cite the narrowest verifiable source that actually supports the claim.** Prefer a segment/product figure over a company total; a primary disclosure over a secondary aggregator; a dated figure with the date shown. If only a broader or weaker figure exists, state the claim at that weaker strength – do not round up the confidence. Show it, do not only prefer it: every external figure in a reply or doc carries its source (a link, or the document name with its page and date).

4. **Flag, don't assert, when unverified.** If the precise number can't be confirmed, write "UNVERIFIED – needs validation" and state the best available weaker fact, rather than presenting a convenient number as fact. Distinguish independent data from self-reported/company-PR data inline.

5. **Actively challenge convenient numbers – especially ones that help the argument.** When a figure makes the case look strong, that is exactly when to double-check its scope and source. Ask "what is the strongest reason this number is being misread or over-claimed?" and answer it in the text. A correction that *weakens* the upside (as the Signify fix did – it reinforced the "demand is unvalidated" finding) is a feature, not a failure. Trigger: when a doc's conclusion rests on an external figure, write one sentence answering why that figure might be over-claimed before the doc ships.

6. **How to fix when caught:** (a) correct the number/scope in place; (b) re-anchor on the narrowest real evidence that does exist (e.g. a specific competitor's product-line revenue rather than a parent total); (c) propagate the correction to every doc that repeated it (grep the whole doc set for the figure/claim); (d) note the downstream consequence honestly, including if it weakens a prior conclusion.
